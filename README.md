# Brain Health Habit Tracker

A calm, simple habit tracker that helps middle-aged adults (about 40 to 65) adopt small, evidence-based habits that support brain health and memory as they age.

Built as a prototype for a university neuroscience group project (NSCI 140, Group 31) on aging and memory.

## How to use it

1. Download or clone this repo.
2. Double-click `index.html`. It opens in any modern browser with no installation, build step, or account.

Your data is saved only in your own browser (localStorage). Nothing is sent anywhere.

## Features

- **Start small:** pick 1 to 3 habits on your first visit, then add, remove, or create your own later.
- **Daily check-off:** tap a habit in the Today list to mark it done.
- **This week:** a 7-day view for each habit, with your current streak and the percentage of days completed.
- **Kind feedback:** encouraging messages that never shame a missed day.
- **Why this helps:** a short, plain-language explanation for each habit.
- **Reset:** clear all data, with a confirmation step first.
- **Themes:** a settings panel for light, dark, or device appearance and four color options.
- **Risk Factors:** a page showing the 14 modifiable dementia risk factors from the 2024 Lancet Commission, the share of cases linked to each, and the tracker habit that supports it.
- **About This Project:** a second page with the project write-up (course concepts, research evidence, significance, impact) and APA references.
- **Accessible:** 18px base text, WCAG AA contrast, 44px+ tap targets, keyboard and screen reader support, dark mode, and reduced-motion support.

## The habits

| Habit | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Move for 30 minutes | Physical activity supports the hippocampus, a brain region important for memory. |
| Connect with someone | Social connection helps buffer stress and supports brain health. |
| Get 7 or more hours of sleep | Sleep helps the brain consolidate memories. |
| Take a calming break | Managing chronic stress helps protect memory-related brain regions. |
| Learn something new | Novelty and challenge help keep brain connections active. |
| Check in on your health | Managing blood pressure, hearing, and vision in midlife is linked to lower dementia risk. |

The habits are inspired by research on brain health, including the 2024 Lancet Commission report on dementia prevention, intervention, and care (Livingston et al., *The Lancet*, 2024).
